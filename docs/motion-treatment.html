Signal not Noise · the visual narrative · before we build

One notice, followed from applause to evidence.

The arc is settled. This page is about how it moves: the set, the camera, the seven moves the screen is allowed to make, and the rhythm of live moments, montages and black holds that stops it feeling like a deck. Every frame below plays on a loop so you can feel the timing. Click a frame to replay it.

Decisions carried in: PM-speak throughout, a hand-driven needle instead of a microphone, the context beat cut, and a planted example wherever the room feeds the talk.

Four more challenges, now that the story holds

Follow one notice through the whole film.

Films follow a character. Ours is "Annual leave needs 4 weeks' notice". It gets cheered in minute two, exposed in minute three, stamped FAIL in minute thirteen, passes in minute twenty, and ships behind the strictest gate at the end. The room watches one thing change, which is how they'll retell it.

So: the card for this notice is never redrawn. It persists between beats, and the set builds up around it.

One needle, not two meters and a dial.

Two small meters plus a separate applause dial is three devices for one idea. A single needle that swings from VIBES to EVIDENCE does all of it: the room pushes it to Vibes in the opening, and every piece of proof nudges it the other way, until the close.

So: the needle is the only persistent HUD. It is the scoreboard above the stage, and it is the applause-o-meter, zoomed, in beat 1.3.

Chapter cards instead of a loop glyph in the corner.

Four dots in a corner carry nothing from row twelve. The loop earns its place as a full-screen chapter card: the path draws itself, the next station lights, two seconds, then the movement starts. Like a film's chapter titles.

So: the loop appears five times, large, each time one station further round. The last time it completes the lap.

"Tireless" needs a montage, not another stamping.

Watching one answer get four stamps shows "judged". It doesn't show "every answer, every time". A ten-second montage of thirty notices flicking through the judges, with a counter spinning and the room's own notices in the stream, shows it.

So: 3.1 ends in a montage, then everything slows for the leave notice. Fast-then-slow is the film move that sells scale.

The set

One space, fixed positions, for the whole talk. The room learns where things live once: what we told it on the left, the notice in the middle, what good looks like above, judgement below, the needle at the top. No beat moves furniture; beats add it, remove it, or push the camera in on it.

The seven moves

A small vocabulary, used consistently, so each move means one thing. If a beat needs an eighth move, the beat is probably wrong.

Typea person is saying thisText appears letter by letter with a caret: the instructions, the room's rules, the feedback notes. Never used for machine output.
Dealthe machine produced thisCards slide in from the left with a slight rotation, one after another, like a hand being dealt. Five tries, the montage stream.
Stampa check happenedA verdict lands oversized and settles with a small overshoot. One stamp at a time, left to right, with a half-second gap so each reason can be read.
Exposelook hereThe camera pushes in on the card, everything else dims, and the changed words get an outline. The only zoom in the talk.
Needlejudgement movedThe dial's needle swings with a slight overshoot. By hand at 1.3; by itself, a few degrees, each time evidence arrives.
Flythis belongs over thereAn existing card moves to a new home: answers into wall columns, rules down into the judgement slot. The object is the same object; the room sees it travel.
Holdremember thisCut to black. One line, large. Nothing moves for three seconds. Every movement ends on one.

The rhythm

Twenty-three minutes, read as a film strip. Live is a human operating the thing in real time. Montage is fast and pre-recorded. Room is the audience doing something. Hold is black with one line. The pattern is deliberate: no two live stretches run back to back without a hold, and every montage is followed by something slow.

Building the motion

None of this needs an animation library. It needs a small, strict system so every beat uses the same seven moves, and a runtime that can reset and replay a beat when you press ←.

The set
One Stage component with fixed slots: needle, note (left), card (centre), rules (above), judgement (below), wall (right, off-canvas). Each scene says which slots are filled. Slots keep their DOM between scenes so objects persist instead of re-mounting. This is what makes "the same card" the same card.
The moves
Seven CSS classes with one transition each (the ones this page uses), a FLIP helper for fly (measure, move, invert, play), a typewriter hook for type, and a needle store with a tween. Expose is a transform on the card plus a dim on siblings. Reduced motion collapses every move to a fade.
The beats
Each scene is a list of steps; → plays the next step, ← rewinds one. A step is "enter these, exit those, move the needle to N, type this, run this (replay or live)". Steps are data in the variant files, like the clicks are today. The presenter view shows the current step's line and the next step's.
The chapter cards
One Loop component, used five times, with the lit station and "draw the path" as props. The SVG path draws with stroke-dashoffset, which this page already does.
The montage
A recorded run played back at 300ms per row, with the room's rows judged live and inserted when their verdicts come back, or dropped on timeout. The counter is just row count and elapsed time.
Order of work
Set and needle first (every beat uses them), then the 1.2 → 1.4 chain end to end so you can rehearse the hook early, then rules → stamps → montage, then the wall, then the bins and bars. The chapter cards and holds last; they are cheap.

What I'd like your eye on before building: the three frames that carry the most, which are 1.4 (the rewind and push-in), 3.1 (rules descending into stamps, then the montage) and 2.5 (the fly-in and the rain). If those three feel right on this page, the rest follows the same grammar.
