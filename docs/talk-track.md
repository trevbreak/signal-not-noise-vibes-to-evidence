## 1 · Fooled

1. “Hands up if an AI demo blew your mind this year.” Wait for the hands.

### 1.2 · Build it in a minute

1. “Nobody reads the delivery team's notices. The brief: make them sound like the one person everyone definitely listens to. The product manager.” The instructions type themselves. “That's the whole product. No code.”
2. “Good morning team, lunch is provided today.” Read the answer in your best stand-up voice.
3. “The coffee machine is broken.” Let the laugh land.
4. “And one off our HR wall.” Read it fast and move on: this is the one that fools them. Don't linger.

## 2 · Say what good is

### 2.1 · Chapter: the loop with a gap

1. “Build, measure, learn. You know this loop.”
2. “Here's what we did. We built, we ran it, we hoped.”
3. “We never said what good looks like. So we can't measure. So we can't learn. Let's fill that gap. Together.”

### 2.2 · The room writes the rules

1. “Stop telling it what to do. Say what a good answer looks like. What must never change when we rewrite a notice? I'll start you off.” *(keys: E retype the card in the room's words)*
2. “What else? Shout.” E to retype the card in their words; Enter to keep it. *(keys: E retype · Enter keep)*
3. “Good. What else?” *(keys: E retype · Enter keep)*
4. “And it still has to sound like a PM, or nobody reads it.” *(keys: E retype · Enter keep)*
5. “Two of these are different. Break the number or the rule and it doesn't matter how good the rest is: instant fail. In eval-speak, an invariant. You'll only hear that word once.”
6. “Anything else goes here, for later. Writing down ‘not now’ is a decision too.” *(keys: E type their extras)*

### 2.3 · Be the judge

1. “Now mark some papers against your rules. Hands up: is A good or bad?” Press 1 (mostly good) to 5 (mostly bad), or → for the usual. *(keys: 1–5 the room's split)*
2. Read the reason. “Still a never.” *(keys: 1–5 adjust)*
3. “B?” Hands. Expect a split. *(keys: 1–5 the room's split)*
4. Let the split sit. Don't resolve it yet. *(keys: 1–5 adjust)*
5. “C?” *(keys: 1–5 the room's split)*
6. “Reversed. Easy.” *(keys: 1–5 adjust)*

### 2.4 · The split

1. “Look at B again. Is ‘let's avoid’ a ‘never’?”
2. “That's not a language question. It's a product decision, and the people who know the work make it. Keep the borderline ones, and write the argument down.”

### 2.5 · The golden wall

1. “What you just did has a name: golden examples. Your three go up first.”
2. “Then we do it again. And again.” The rest arrive. “Notice which column is biggest.”
3. “Good examples show what to copy. Bad ones, with the reason, show what to avoid. Borderline ones show where your rules are still fuzzy. Fourteen here. Hundreds in a real product.”

### 2.5 · Hold: bad examples teach

1. Let it sit.

## 3 · Make it tireless

### 3.0 · Chapter: measure

1. “You just marked three answers. Production is ten thousand a day.”

### 3.1 · Your rules become judges

1. “Here are your rules.”
2. “Each one becomes a judge. A plain rule: is the 4 still there? No AI needed. AI judges that read your rule and your examples. And people, for the ones that split. Cheapest first.”
3. “Every answer. Every judge.” The montage runs, with the room's own notices in it. Let it play.
4. “That's an eval: your rules, applied to every answer, every time.”

### 3.2 · The notice that fooled you, stamped

1. “Now slow down. The one that fooled you.”
2. Stamps land one at a time. Read each reason. “The rule judge: the 4 is gone. Notice period halved. Told the team. And it passed ‘sounds like a PM’ with flying colours.”
3. “Room, last stamp. Thumbs?” Wait for them, then ↑ or ↓ (→ for thumbs down). *(keys: ↑ thumbs up · ↓ thumbs down)*
4. The room's stamp lands, then: “Fail. Nobody fooled this time.” *(keys: ↑ ↓ change the room's call)*
5. “Version 3 doesn't add rules. It describes what good looks like, using your rules and your examples.” The answer rewrites and the stamps land again. “Room: thumbs this time?” ↑ or ↓. *(keys: ↑ thumbs up · ↓ thumbs down)*

### 3.2 · Better, and proven

1. “Five tries each, every judge. One line: one in five. More rules: one in five. Describing good: four in five.”
2. “That's the first time in this talk we can say it got better, and prove it. Look at the needle.”

## 4 · Check the checker

### 4.0 · Chapter: learn

1. “The judges are marking everything now. Who's checking the judges?” Beat. “You are.”

### 4.1 · A judge is wrong

1. “Here's one from the release notes.”
2. Stamps land. “Three passes. One fail: nothing added.”
3. “Read why. Its guide bans the words ‘tech debt’, because PMs use them to wave work away. But this is the one honest sentence in the whole demo.”
4. “Agree with the judge, or disagree? Hands.” 1 (agree) to 5 (disagree), or → for the usual. *(keys: 1–5 agree … disagree)*
5. “The judge checked the wrong thing. You just overruled it.” *(keys: 1–5 adjust)*

### 4.2 · Two kinds of feedback

1. “Two moves, and the whole discipline sits on them.”
2. “Agree with a fail: the answer really is bad, so it's a note for the instructions.”
3. “Disagree: the judge is wrong, so it's a note for the judge.”
4. “And the note says why, in plain words. ‘Wrong’ teaches nothing. The reason teaches everything.”

### 4.3 · Feedback in, proof out

1. “We hand both notes back. We never say how to fix anything, only what was wrong and why.”
2. “It rewrote the instructions…”
3. “…and the judge.”
4. “Did it actually get better? Same notices, same judges, fair fight. One in six to four in six. And the judge now agrees with the room on every one.”

### 4.3 · Chapter: the loop, closed

1. “Define, build, measure, learn. That's the loop. Closed.”

### 4.4 · Months, not minutes

1. “Every notice, every version, every judge. We ran this before the talk. More rules: no better than one line. Describing good: the jump.”
2. “None of them is done. Getting to 80% takes an afternoon. The last 20 points take months, and the evals tell you which months were worth it.”

## 5 · Ship it?

### 5.1 · Ship it? Depends who it's for

1. “So, do we ship it? Depends who it's for.”
2. “A Friday drinks email: ninety percent, and vibes are fine.”
3. “Customer marketing: ninety-five, plus a brand review.”
4. “The leave policy: a hundred percent on the instant fails, and a human signs off.”
5. “Same app. Three bars. Product owns the rules, the people who know the work own the labels, delivery owns the gate. Set the bar before you look at the results.”

### 5.2 · The needle comes home

2. “Same notice we started with. Five judges, a human's tick, and the strictest gate. You wrote the rules, you marked the papers, you overruled the judge.”
